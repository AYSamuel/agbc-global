# W4.10 · Updating the app from inside the app

**Status:** BUILT, 2026-09-08, all three slices. Written the same day, after Ayo
asked why an update still means "go and find us in the store".

**What cannot be verified until the next release, and why:** everything here.
Play's in-app updates work only on a build the Play Store itself installed, so an
EAS APK reports "no update available" forever, and `expo-in-app-updates` is a
native module that no dev client and no binary in review carries. See section 4.

**Refs:** `21` §113 (rollback), §117-119 (versioning, OTA, forced update), §122
(cadence), §133 (EAS meters) · `18` line 91 (the gate, already ticked) ·
`docs/runbooks/store-submission.md` · `~/.claude/standards/mobile.md` "Releases" ·
`apps/mobile/src/features/update-gate/`

---

## 1. What exists, and what does not

**Built (W1.2):** the hard floor. `ForcedUpdateGate` reads
`app_config.minimum_supported_version` on launch, caches it, and replaces the app
with `UpdateRequiredScreen` when the running binary is below it. That is the
"you may not continue" case, and it is the only update mechanism the app has.

**Not built, though `21` names both:**

- **`expo-in-app-updates`** (`21` §119, and the gate's own comment: "NOT wired
  yet ... a store-submission polish item (tracked, not dropped)"). Nothing tells
  a member that a newer version exists unless they are below the floor, and the
  floor's screen sends them to a store listing rather than installing anything.
- **`expo-updates`** is not installed at all, so there is no OTA. The groundwork
  is half-laid and easy to misread as working: `runtimeVersion: { policy:
  'fingerprint' }` is set in `app.config.js` and `eas.json` carries
  development/preview/production channels, but no updates runtime exists to
  serve or apply anything.

## 2. The decision: store-only, no OTA (Ayo, 2026-09-08)

`21` has assumed OTA since it was written: §118 defines it, §122's cadence is
"fortnightly store train early; OTA between trains", §113's first rollback step
is "OTA republish previous update", and §133 budgets EAS Update's 1,000 MAU free
tier against a congregation that would exceed it in the first OTA month.

**That assumption is dropped here.** Every update rides the store train.

Why it is defensible: the experience Ayo asked for is achievable without OTA,
because the modern install-and-relaunch flow on Android is Play's own in-app
update API, not EAS Update. OTA buys exactly one thing on top: **fixes that skip
review**.

What the choice costs, stated so nobody rediscovers it in an incident:

1. **No same-day fixes.** A typo, a wrong colour, a screen that breaks on a
   Sunday morning: each is a build, a submission, a review (Play hours to days,
   Apple around a day) and then a member who has to update.
2. **Rollback loses its fast half.** `21` §113's "OTA republish previous update"
   is gone; recovery is halting the rollout and shipping a higher version, which
   is exactly what `mobile.md` warns is the only store-train recovery.
3. **Build minutes carry the load.** EAS Free is 15 Android + 15 iOS builds a
   month, and every fix now needs one.

Against that: no $19/mo, one mechanism rather than two, no drift between an
OTA'd JS bundle and the binary in the store, and no judgement calls about
Apple's "review-relevant features via OTA" grey area.

**Not a one-way door.** `expo-updates` can be added later onto the channels
`eas.json` already declares, at the cost of one more store release.

## 3. What gets built

`expo-in-app-updates` (0.12.0, published 2026-06-07) does both platforms in the
way each platform allows, which is why it is the whole implementation:

- **Android:** the native Play in-app updates library. A **flexible** update
  downloads in the background and asks to restart; an **immediate** update takes
  over the screen until it is installed.
- **iOS:** the iTunes Search API to learn the App Store's current version, then
  the App Store opened in a modal, "since iOS does not have any in-app update
  solution" (the package's own words, and the platform's).

### The three states, and which mechanism owns each

| State | Who decides | What the member sees |
|---|---|---|
| Below `minimum_supported_version` | our own `app_config` floor | today's blocking screen, but its button now INSTALLS on Android instead of linking out; iOS still opens the App Store |
| A newer version exists, not mandatory | Play (Android) / iTunes lookup (iOS) | a quiet in-app notice with an Update action, dismissible, at most once a day |
| Two or more releases behind | Play's own staleness counter (section 5) | Play's IMMEDIATE flow on Android: the screen is taken until it installs. iOS gets the ordinary notice, because Apple has no takeover flow to escalate INTO (section 7) |
| Up to date | either | nothing at all |

### Slices

1. **The module and the optional notice. DONE.** `expo-in-app-updates` behind one
   guarded require (`inAppUpdates.ts`, the `expoNotifications.ts` pattern: this
   module is brand new, so EVERY client in existence takes the null branch until
   the next EAS build). The check runs on mount and on every foreground, and is
   skipped outright on a day already answered. `UpdateNotice.tsx` is the sheet,
   mounted at the root INSIDE `ForcedUpdateGate` (a binary already stopped by the
   floor has no business being offered a choice) and behind all four other
   overlays. Android: `startUpdate(false)`, the flexible flow, whose install
   listener calls `completeUpdate()` on its own and relaunches the app. iOS: the
   App Store modal, inert for now (below). Copy in four languages under
   `common.updateNotice`.
2. **The floor gets teeth, and the escalation. DONE.** `UpdateRequiredScreen`'s
   button runs `startUpdate(true)`, Play's immediate flow, and falls back to the
   store link, which is unchanged behaviour on iOS and on any build Play cannot
   serve. `shouldTakeOver()` sends a member far enough behind straight into the
   same immediate flow instead of raising the sheet.
3. **The docs. DONE.** `21` sections 7-9 (the rollback line, versioning, OTA,
   forced update, a new bullet for the notice, cadence, the EAS meter), `01`
   sections 1 and 6, `23` (b), `18`'s launch checklist, and
   `store-submission.md` (the OTA gap becomes a decision, plus a new section 4b
   for what a release does and does not have to do).

## 4. What has to be true before this can be verified

- **A new binary is required either way.** `expo-in-app-updates` is a native
  module, so nothing here reaches the versionCode 22 currently in review. This
  ships in the release after it.
- **Android in-app updates only work on a Play-installed app.** A sideloaded EAS
  APK reports no update available, always. Verification needs an internal
  testing track install and a HIGHER versionCode published to it, which means
  the first real test of this feature is also the first release after it.
- **iOS cannot be verified at all yet**: no iPhone in the project, and the
  iTunes lookup needs a live App Store record.

## 5. Decided, and still open

**Decided by Ayo, 2026-09-08.**

- **The soft notice appears at most once a day**, not on every cold start. A
  member who is not ready to update should not be asked again three launches
  later; a day is long enough to be a reminder rather than nagging. Persisted
  locally, like every other "we have already said this" flag in the app
  (`features/notifications/ask.ts` is the pattern).
- **Two releases behind escalates to the immediate flow.** The dismissible
  notice is for the release you have not got round to; being two behind is
  treated as a version we no longer want in the wild, and Play takes the screen
  until it is installed. This does NOT touch the floor: the floor still means
  "may not run at all", and it stays where `18` line 91 leaves it.

**A unit has to be chosen for "two releases behind", and neither obvious
candidate works.**

- `versionCode` counts BUILDS, not releases: 21 and 22 were two builds of the
  same 1.0.0 release (`eas.json` sets `autoIncrement`), so counting it would
  escalate a member who is perfectly current.
- Semver distance is not a count either: 1.0.0 to 1.0.1 to 1.1.0 is two
  releases, and 1.0.0 to 1.2.0 might be one.

**The release counter was recommended and then NOT built.** It would carry an
integer in `app.config.js` `extra`, bumped once per store train, mirrored in
`app_config`, and "behind by two" would be subtraction. What killed it while
building: it buys a unit that **reads the same on both platforms, and the
escalation is Android-only**, because Apple has no takeover flow at all. So the
generality would never be used, while the costs are real: a second `app_config`
key, a hand-bumped integer every train has to remember, and a member locked into
a Play install screen if anyone forgets it twice.

**What shipped instead is Play's own `clientVersionStalenessDays`**, the days
since the Play Store app ON THAT DEVICE learnt a newer version existed, at a
threshold of **28 days**. The arithmetic that makes it mean "two releases": `21`
section 122 puts the store train at a fortnight, and the counter starts when the
NEXT version became visible, so 28 days means that version has been out for two
trains and at least one more has shipped behind it. If the cadence changes, the
number changes with it, and that is the whole maintenance burden.

**It can never fire on our say-so**, which is the safety property that matters
when the consequence is taking somebody's screen: Play must report the update,
Play must allow the immediate flow, and Play must supply the staleness, with a
missing staleness declining to escalate rather than reading as zero. Play's own
update priority (4 or higher, set per release) escalates immediately and is the
church's lever for "this one cannot wait".

**The floor is now set** (2026-09-08): production carries
`{"ios": "1.0.0", "android": "1.0.0"}`, replacing the seed's `0.0.0`. It blocks
nobody, because 1.0.0 is the published version, and that is the only safe value
while it is the only version in the wild. Ayo asked for `1.0.0+22` and it cannot
work: `parseVersion` accepts `x.y.z` only, the gate reads `expoConfig.version`
(`1.0.0`) and never `versionCode`, and an unparseable floor FAILS OPEN, so it
would have gated nothing while looking set.

**Still open, and the only thing this item leaves behind**

1. **Should the gate learn build numbers?** A `x.y.z+n` floor, with the running
   build read from `expo-application`'s `nativeBuildVersion`, is what "retire
   build 22 now that 23 is out" needs. Small, and worth a slice here. One catch
   to accept: it only ever bites builds that CONTAIN that code, so build 22 stays
   gate-able by semver alone, forever.

  forced-update and cadence bullets plus a new one for the notice; section 9's

  optional Play priority lever) and why the feature cannot be tested before the
  next one.
- `02`: nothing. `app_config` keeps its shape; this item adds no column, and the
  release counter that would have added a key was not built (section 5).

## 7. What is on screen, and what is deliberately not

Two frames, both in `entry-flow.html` under `W4.10`, both approved 2026-09-08.

- **UPDATE-NOTICE**, the sheet. New.
- **UPDATE-REQUIRED**, the wall, drawn at last as it actually ships. It was built
  at W1.2 with **no frame at all** (its own file said so) and W4.10 changes its
  button, so it finally has a reference to be diffed against.

**The escalated state has no frame on purpose.** At two releases behind, the
interface is Play's own immediate-update screen, which is Google's and not ours,
so there is nothing of ours to draw.

**And there is deliberately no iOS variant of it.** The table above once promised
iOS "the notice undismissible instead", and that was dropped while building: it
would be a third, unframed surface, for a platform with no App Store id, no
build, and no way to verify it, gating on a check that today always answers
"nothing new". iOS keeps the ordinary dismissible notice and the wall, which is
what Apple's ceiling allows anyway.
