# W4.15 · Share as a picture

**Status:** FRAMES COMPOSED, AWAITING AYO'S APPROVAL, 2026-09-12. **No code is
written and none should be** until the frames in `entry-flow.html` `W4.15` are
approved: the house rule is mockup-first, and this item is almost entirely a
design item with a thin mechanism under it.

**Where it came from:** Ayo, 2026-09-12. "The share button currently is text
only, but we need it to be in picture form, so if a person wants to share to
WhatsApp it is in picture form." Not a new idea: `07` line 54 has said "Share
renders a branded verse image/text" since it was written, and
`features/home/VerseCard.tsx` has carried the deferral note in its header since
W1.2, naming the blocker correctly.

**Refs:** `07` §40, §52, §54 (the verse card, the WEB translation, the phasing) ·
`09` §21-27 (compose, consent), §73 (multilingual UGC and OS-share translation),
§26 (the 2000/1000 character ceilings) · `11` §24 (event share) · `08` §25
(player share) · `04` §67, §132, §142 (share on the player, the event, the
branch) · `10` §31 (milestones) · `20` §Photos, and Article 9 generally ·
`02` §Storage (`testimony-photos`, `event-images`, `sermon-artwork`) ·

## 1. What exists today

Nine call sites, every one of them plain text, all routed through one small
module. The module is good and survives this item; what changes is what it is
handed.

| # | Surface | Where | Today |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Daily verse | `src/features/home/VerseCard.tsx:27` | `Share.share({message})` inline |
| 2 | Testimony card, Family feed | `src/features/family/FamilyListPane.tsx:189` | `shareText(testimonyShareText(...))` |
| 3 | Testimony highlight, Home | `app/(tabs)/home.tsx:738` | same |
| 4 | Testimony detail | `app/testimony/[id].tsx:322` | `shareToWhatsApp(...)`, a `wa.me` link |
| 5 | Prayer detail | `app/prayer/[id].tsx:98` | `shareText(testimonyShareText(...))` |
| 6 | Event detail | `app/event/[id].tsx:180` | `shareText(...)` |
| 7 | Branch info | `app/branch/[id].tsx:172` | `shareText(...)` |
| 8 | Sermon player | `app/sermon/[id].tsx:278` | `Share.share({message})` inline |
| 9 | Milestone celebration | `src/features/rhythm/MilestoneCelebration.tsx:86` | `shareText(t('rhythm:celebrateShareText'))` |

Two of the nine (1 and 8) bypass `share.ts` and call React Native directly.
Folding them in is part of this item and is worth doing on its own merits.

## 2. The four decisions (Ayo, 2026-09-12)

1. **A preview sheet, not a silent hand-off.** Share opens `SHARE-PREVIEW`,
   which shows the card and then hands it to the OS sheet. Rejected: going
   straight to the share sheet (nobody ever sees what they sent, and there is
   nowhere for a text fallback to live) and a long-press for the picture
   (undiscoverable).
2. **Square only, 1080 × 1080.** One frame per surface. It survives a WhatsApp
   chat, a WhatsApp status, an Instagram feed and a Facebook post. A 9:16 story
   card is cropped hard by the most common share of all, and doing both doubles
   the design and the layouts for a second format nobody asked for.
3. **All nine surfaces**, prayer requests included, after the privacy flag in
   section 5 was read.
4. **The footer is the wordmark, `agbcglobal.com` and a QR**, on every card.
   Amended the same day, when Ayo asked the question that undid my framing of it:
   *is the footer clickable?* It is not. The card is a PNG, so the printed address
   carries the information but not the route, and a recipient has to read it,
   leave the chat and type it. **The QR is the only element on the card anybody
   can act on**, scannable straight off the received image by Lens on Android or
   the Photos app on iOS. Rejected: mark-only (leaves a stranger nothing at all)
   and QR-instead-of-address (anyone unwilling to scan is left with nothing
   readable). Costs no new native module, see section 8.
   **It points at `https://agbcglobal.com` for now** (Ayo, 2026-09-12). "For now"
   is the operative half: the church's front door is the safe destination while
   there is nothing better, and the two things that would be better are a page
   that routes to the app store and a per-content deep link, the second of which
   the website cannot support today (it has no page per testimony or event). So
   this is a destination chosen against what exists, not a preference, and it
   should be revisited the moment the website grows either. **The build keeps it
   as ONE exported constant** next to the card component, so changing it later is
   a one-line edit rather than a hunt through eleven layouts.

## 3. The constraint that shaped the card

**`expo-sharing` sends a file and nothing else.** Its entire options type is
`mimeType`, `dialogTitle`, `UTI` and `anchor`: there is no message field on any
platform. React Native's own `Share.share({url})` is iOS-only for files, so on
Android `expo-sharing` is the only road and **no caption, no link and no app name
can travel beside the picture**.

Everything a recipient needs in order to find us therefore has to be printed on
the card. That is not a styling preference; it is why the footer exists, why it is
on all eleven cards, and why **the way back had to become a QR rather than text**:
printed words carry the information but not the route (decision 4).

It also means the move from text to picture is a **loss** as well as a gain, and
section 6 is about paying that back.

## 4. The cards

Eleven artboards, seven kinds and four variants, all drawn in `entry-flow.html`
under `W4.15` at 360 × 360 for 1080 × 1080 (one css px there is three real ones).

**Common anatomy, so eleven cards read as one family:**

- **A kicker row**: a 16px gold stroke icon and an uppercase label naming the
  kind. Lifted wholesale from `.verse .row1`, which the app already draws.
- **A middle**: the content, vertically centred on a flat ground and
  **bottom-aligned on a photo ground** (section 7).
- **A footer**: a hairline, then the gold mark with `AGBC Global` and the address
  stacked beside it on the left, and the QR on the right.

**The grounds, and why each:**

| Ground | Cards | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cream/gold | verse | `.verse`'s own values, scaled. A member taps a cream card and a cream card lands in the chat |
| Ink gradient | testimony, prayer, branch, event with no picture | the app's band colour, so a forwarded post still looks like it came from here |
| Photo + scrim | testimony with a photo, event with its picture, message | the picture is the card; the scrim is `.photo::before`'s, strengthened |
| Gold | milestone | the only gold-ground card in the app, because it is the only card that is purely glad |

**The artwork rule for a message is not new**: its own artwork, else the YouTube
thumbnail, else the branded gradient, exactly as W3.1 slice 5 and W4.9 already
settled for the player, the rails, MY-LIST and the lock screen.

**A testimony is marked as a quotation with real curly quotes**, the way
`.testi .body` and `testimonyShareText()` already do it. A prayer request gets
none: it is not a quotation, it is somebody asking.

**The milestone card carries the member's FULL profile name** (Ayo, 2026-09-12),
where every other card uses the feed's abbreviated `author_name` shape
("Sarah O."). That is not an inconsistency to tidy up later. It is the one card
where **the subject and the sharer are always the same person**: a testimony can
be shared by anybody who sees it, so the name on it belongs to someone who did
not choose to send it, whereas a milestone can only ever be shared by the person
it happened to. There is no third party to protect, and a name a member is giving
about themselves is theirs to give in full. It reads as a signature, which is what
it is.

**A contrast pass ran over the cards and caught four of their own colours**
(2026-09-12). A gold ground is the brightest surface in the app, and the trap is
that it looks *more* legible than it measures, so nothing prompts you to check.
Against the gradient's darker end the milestone's kicker was 3.33:1, its branch
line 3.77 and its url 3.77, all under WCAG 1.4.3's 4.5 for text at these sizes;
the cream card's url was 3.72 for the same reason. All four are now 4.78 to 5.01.
W4.7 ran this pass across the app and could not reach here, because these cards
did not exist. **The `shareCard` token group must carry the corrected values**, or
the build reintroduces what the frames just fixed.

## 5. The prayer request, flagged and then chosen

**The flag, raised before the decision and recorded here so it is not
re-litigated:** a prayer request can carry Article 9 health data (`20`), it can
be posted anonymously, and a picture travels further than text and cannot be
edited back once it is in somebody's camera roll. Ayo read that and included it.

**What the design does about it, which is not nothing:**

- The card carries **only what the text share already carries**. No Glory count,
  no reaction, no date, no category.
- The ask is a **line, not a button**. A picture has no controls, and drawing one
  that cannot be pressed is a lie the card tells in every chat it lands in.
- **An anonymous request drops the branch, and this is a deliberate departure
  from what the app shows in its own feed.** In the app, "A member" sits inside a
  branch-scoped feed the reader already had to be in. On a card that gets
  forwarded, naming the branch narrows an anonymous and possibly medical request
  to one congregation of a few dozen people, which is most of the way to naming
  the author. The words stay, "A member" stays, the branch goes.
- "A member" is `family:aMember`, the same words the composer offers and the same
  words a departed author's kept post carries, so the card never reveals **which**
  kind of anonymity this is.

## 6. Accessibility, and why the text share does not die

**A picture cannot be read aloud by a screen reader and cannot be translated by
the person receiving it.** `09` line 73 makes OS-share translation the v1 answer
for a post written in a language the reader does not speak, and this item would
quietly take that away from every share.

So **"Send as text instead" is on the happy sheet, not only the failure path.**
It is a ghost button rather than a link so it is a real target, and it needs
`hitSlop` to reach the 44dp floor rather than growing and breaking the frame, the
same trick `VerseCard`'s chip already uses.

**The card does not follow the device font scale, and cannot.** Every other
surface in this app scales to 200% and must; this one is a fixed 1080 canvas and
at 200% the words would simply leave the picture. The card renders with font
scaling off and fits by the ladder below instead. The member's own settings are
honoured by the other button on the sheet, which produces text their reader can
size, speak and translate.

**The auto-fit ladder**, which `09` §26's ceilings force: a testimony may run to
2000 characters and a prayer request to 1000, and nothing near that fits on a
1080 square at a readable size. The quote steps down through three display sizes
(75 / 63 / 54 real px) looking for one that fits; when the smallest still
overflows, the text is cut on a word boundary and the card **says so** in gold:
"Read it all in the app". A truncated quote that pretends to be whole is the one
outcome not allowed, because this is somebody's testimony and a card that ends it
mid-thought misrepresents them to everyone who receives it.

**The cut lands near 190 characters, and that number was measured, not chosen.**
See section 7.

## 7. What the render caught, and it caught three things

The frames were opened in Chrome before they were called done, per the house
rule. Three defects were invisible in the HTML and obvious in the render:

1. **The long-testimony card pushed its own footer off the bottom.** The first
   draft asserted a longer cut, rendered, and overflowed. Five lines at the
   smallest size is what a 1080 square actually holds once the kicker, the cut
   line, the attribution and the footer have taken their share. **The build fits
   by measuring the laid-out text, not by counting characters**; 190 is the
   sanity check, not the mechanism.
2. **The decorative oversized open quote read as a grey smudge.** At any alpha
   subtle enough to sit behind the words it looked like a rendering artefact, and
   at any alpha that read as a quote it fought them. Removed, and replaced with
   the real curly quotes the app already uses.
3. **Photo cards centred their text at 42% height, where the scrim is 62%**, over
   a photograph nobody has vetted. Moved to the foot, where the scrim is 94%.

## 8. How it gets built

**Two native modules, and they are the whole reason this was deferred at W1.2:**
`react-native-view-shot` (`npx expo install react-native-view-shot`) and
`expo-sharing`. Both trip the dev-client native fence, so both dev clients go
stale and neither the sheet nor the cards can be seen on a device until an EAS
rebuild. Guard the imports so every screen degrades to today's text share until
then, the `Gradient.tsx` / `CopyRow.tsx` pattern, and get Ayo's go-ahead for the
builds.

**The preview is the output, not a picture of it.** One `<ShareCard>` is mounted
off-screen at the real card size, captured to a PNG, and the sheet then shows
**that file** in an `<Image>`. This costs nothing and buys the one guarantee
worth having: the preview cannot drift from what is sent, because they are the
same bytes. It also makes the "preparing" state real rather than a courtesy.

**The one technical unknown, to be settled by a spike in slice 1 and not guessed
at here.** `captureRef`'s `width`/`height` options are documented on Expo without
saying whether they set the render size or resize the result, and the behaviour of
capturing a view positioned outside the window differs by platform. Slice 1
settles, on the device, which of these gives a crisp 1080 PNG:

- render the card at 1080 logical px off-screen and capture it 1:1, or
- render at a size that fits the screen and let `captureRef` scale up, which
  risks a soft card and would be visible immediately, or
- render at 360 and capture at `pixelRatio` 3.

**The QR costs no native module.** `react-native-qrcode-svg` is pure JavaScript
(`qrcode` plus `prop-types`) over `react-native-svg`, which is already linked here
for the map, so it adds no EAS build on top of the two this item already owes.
Verified against the published package rather than assumed. The code in the frames
is real and encodes `https://agbcglobal.com`, so it can be scanned rather than
taken on trust.

**Tokens.** The card's colours are literals in the mockup because the mockup is
the token source, but the app must not hard-code them: add a `shareCard` group to
`packages/shared` theme tokens, taken from the mockup's values verbatim, the same
way `verseCard` already exists. **The card is deliberately not theme-aware** and
that must be written on the token group, or the next session will "fix" it by
adding a dark variant.

**Copy.** Every string is new and needs EN/DE/NL/FR: seven kicker labels, "Will
you pray?", "Read it all in the app", seven sheet titles, "Share", "Send as text
instead", "Send as text", "Try the picture again", "Getting it ready…" and the
error sentence. **The card is baked in the SHARER's UI language**, which is the
only language available at capture time; note it, because it is a question
somebody will ask.

**Analytics: `content_shared` (Ayo, 2026-09-12; ADR 0020 amended the same day).**
There has never been a share event at all, so the app's only organic growth
surface has been unmeasured since launch. Added to
`packages/shared/src/analytics/events.ts` now, as `deferred` with W4.15 as owner,
because that file is the tracking plan rather than a record of what is wired:
"adding a key here is the decision; wiring the call site is the consequence."

It carries `content_kind` (one of the seven card kinds) and `sent_as`, and
**`sent_as` is a three-way union rather than a format plus a `fell_back` flag**:
`text` is a member preferring words, `text_after_failure` is a member handed
words because the capture failed, and those are a verdict on the feature and a
verdict on the build respectively. A boolean pair would let a call site claim
both at once. The third value is also **the only monitoring this item gets for a
failure mode no test here can see**, since rasterising a view is native work on
hardware we do not own: if it is anything but rare in the field, the capture is
broken on real devices.

Slice 1 owes the compile-time assertion that every `ShareKind` the app can raise
appears in `AnalyticsShareKind`, modelled on `assertGateActionsCovered`, so
adding a tenth share surface later fails typecheck instead of shipping unmeasured.

## 9. Slices

1. **The mechanism, behind one surface.** Both modules, guarded; the `ShareCard`
   component and the `shareCard` tokens; the capture spike above; `SHARE-PREVIEW`
   with all three states; the verse card wired to it. One surface end to end
   proves the road, per the build-then-verify rule.
2. **Testimonies and prayer**, which is four of the nine call sites and carries
   the photo ground, the auto-fit ladder, the truncation line and the anonymous
   rule. The heaviest slice, and the wedge.
3. **Event, message and branch**: three photo/ink cards over buckets that already
   exist, plus folding `sermon/[id].tsx` into `share.ts`.
4. **The milestone**, the smallest, and the close-out: the device matrix, both
   themes, max font scale, the longest branch name, and a real send into WhatsApp
   on the physical Android device.

## 10. What cannot be verified before the rebuild

Everything visual. Unit tests pass against any PNG at any path, which is exactly
the shape of W4.8's placeholder-icon lesson: **a phone's home screen is the one
surface no test looks at**, and here the equivalent is the picture that lands in
somebody's WhatsApp. The acceptance test is a real send, from the physical
device, opened in the receiving app, and read at thumbnail size in a chat list.

**And the QR must be scanned AFTER the forward, never before.** The arithmetic is
uncomfortable and is recorded on the frame: at 46px drawn the code is 138px on the
real card and a module is 4.2px, which is comfortable, but chat apps recompress and
at an 800px downscale a module falls to 3.1px, at or under what a scanner resolves.
So scanning the PNG we produced proves nothing. Send the card through WhatsApp,
save the received copy, and scan THAT, on a second phone. If it fails, the lever is
the URL rather than the size: 14 bytes or fewer encodes as a 21-module version 1
instead of 25, which is 14% bigger modules for no card space, and `agbcglobal.com`
without the scheme is exactly 14.

## 11. Deferred, and recorded so it is not rediscovered

- **A story format (1080 × 1920).** Decided against, not forgotten. If it comes
  back, it is a second layout per card and a toggle on the sheet.
- **"Save to photos".** Would need `expo-media-library` and a write permission
  for a convenience the OS share sheet already offers via the gallery app.
- **A share library that can carry a caption.** Checked when the footer question
  came up, because a clickable link beside the picture would beat a QR outright.
  `react-native-share` does accept a message and a file together where
  `expo-sharing` cannot, but WhatsApp prioritises one over the other, so on the
  app this church actually shares into, the caption and the image fight and one
  is dropped. It buys no dependable link for a heavier dependency, so the route
  stays IN the picture.
- **A second event for opening the preview sheet and abandoning it.** Rejected
  with `content_shared` itself: the sheet is one tap from a card, so most of its
  abandonment is a mis-tap rather than a verdict, and ADR 0020 decision 5's
  restraint about unlisted events is worth more than the number.

## 12. Open questions for Ayo

1. **Does the mark on the card need to be the real app icon** rendered from
   `scripts/render-app-icon.sh`'s source, rather than the gold "A" tile drawn in
   the frames? The frames use the tile because it is what the mockup has; the
   real mark exists and would be sharper.
2. **Does the QR survive a real forward?** Not a taste question, and not one that
   can be settled from a desktop: see section 10. If it does not, the lever is a
   shorter URL rather than a bigger code, and that trade interacts with the
   destination decided in section 2 (a longer app-store path would make the code
   denser, a short link would make it sparser).
