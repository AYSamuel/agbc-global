# W3.6 · Phase 3 exit audit

The last item in Phase 3. `18`'s exit sentence is two clauses: **members get the right
notifications at the right scope; audio works while driving.** `25`'s entry names three
pieces of work (tier scoping verification, the audio field test, the notification E2E
matrix) and strikes a fourth (the load-test note went with ADR 0021 on 2026-08-16, and is
not to be reinstated).

Reading the specs against the code on 2026-08-29 turned up three findings, one of which
turns this item from a pure audit into an audit plus a backend slice. **Delete once W3.6
lands.**

---

## The three findings

### 1. Three specified notification kinds have no producer at all

`09` §Notifications promises exactly three things: *"Your testimony got Glory reactions.
Someone prayed for your request. Your post was approved / needs changes."* `15`'s tier
table carries the same three, as the Personal-activity tier plus one transactional row.

Everything around them is built, in four languages, on both sides of the wire:

| Piece | Where | State |
|---|---|---|
| `type` values | `20260816120000` CHECK | ✅ `prayer`, `testimony_glory`, `moderation` |
| channel + pref gate | `_shared/pushChannels.ts:64-68` | ✅ `prayer_activity`, `testimony_activity`, `transactional` |
| templates × 4 languages | `_shared/pushTemplates.ts:60,88,111,125` | ✅ `prayer.someone_prayed`, `testimony.glory_batch`, `moderation.approved`, `moderation.changes_needed` |
| foreground rule | `features/notifications/foreground.ts:57` | ✅ tray, no banner |
| NC tint + renderer | `features/notifications/render.ts:95-99` | ✅ `pray` / `glory` / `txn` discs |
| i18n, both sides | `locales/*/notifications.json` | ✅ |
| the switches | NOTIF-PREFS | ✅ |
| the destination | MY-POSTS renders all four statuses incl. `removed` (`myPosts.ts:25`) | ✅ |
| **the caller** | (none) | ❌ **nothing** |

There are exactly two INSERT sites into `public.notifications`: `deliver_notifications()`
(`20260819120000:112`) and the broadcast fan-out (`20260819200000:105`). The callers of
`deliver_notifications` are the four jobs and nothing else. `moderateItem.ts` writes no
notification; `useGlory.ts` writes no notification.

**Why this blocks the exit criterion, rather than being a nice-to-have.**
`prayer_activity` and `testimony_activity` are two of the five switches on NOTIF-PREFS and
**neither one gates anything**, so `15`'s acceptance criterion "prefs actually suppress the
corresponding categories" cannot be true for them. A member is being shown two controls
that do nothing. And `09` line 82's "removed content disappears from public; author
notified with reason" never fires at all.

**Why nobody caught it.** This is the W3.4 shape again ("W3.3 built the push sender and
never built a caller"). W3.3 built the infrastructure; W3.4 owned **scheduled** sends;
W3.5 owned **broadcast and event** sends. Sends triggered by *another member's action* are
a fourth shape and no work item ever claimed it. Per §8's work-item drift rule, W3.6 claims
it and `25` records why.

`registration.confirmed` is a fourth orphan of the same kind, from W2.9 shipping before push
existed; it is **out of scope here** and goes on `18`'s launch checklist. `purchase.added`
is legitimately W4.1's.

Where the scoping claim actually stands in pgTAP today:

The implementation reads correct (`p.branch_id = b.branch_id`, `20260820180000:583`), but

## Decisions taken with Ayo (2026-08-29)

1. **The missing producers are built inside W3.6**, not split into a W3.7 and not deferred.
   Phase 3's exit sentence is "the right notifications at the right scope", and two dead
   switches on a member-facing screen is the clearest possible failure of it. Everything but
   the caller already exists, so this is a caller, not infrastructure.
2. **"Someone prayed for your request" fires on "I prayed", not on "I will pray"**
   (`prayer_intercessions.state = 'prayed'`). It matches the template's past tense exactly
   and tells the truth. The accepted cost: a member who commits and never marks prayed
   produces no notification, which is part of why the three nudges exist.
3. **The moderation notice is a generic push to MY-POSTS, and a removal sends one too.**
   The payload carries title + deep link only; the author reads `rejection_reason` in the
   app. `moderation_note` is **never** used, per `20260803140000` (it is the safeguarding
   record and is invisible to the author by column privilege, not by policy).
4. **Glory collapses per post per HOUR, not per day**, with a settle window so a burst is
   one notification. Chosen over the per-day collapse because per-day both undercounts and
   silently drops: on a testimony that does well the author would be told "3" while eight
   more people responded that she never hears about, which is the opposite of what this
   notification exists to do. Chosen over a daily evening digest because Glory is the app's
   warmest feedback loop and immediacy is doing pastoral work there. `15`'s "collapses per
   post per day" is amended in the same PR. **The window is a parameter**: if a slow trickle
   proves annoying, widening the hour to three or six is a one-line change and no migration.
5. **The E2E matrix runs against PRODUCTION with a fresh preview build.** ~~Local stack
   with the dev client.~~ **Reversed on 2026-08-29, before Slice 3 ran**, when preparing the
   device turned up two facts the original decision did not know. First, there IS no dev
   client on the phone: Track P Phase 4 installed the preview build over it (same
   `applicationId`), so one replaces the other. Second, and decisive, Track P diagnosed
   W3.3's tap gap as "a dev-client artefact", meaning the tap legs work on the preview build
   and did NOT on the dev client. Running the tap matrix on a dev client would be testing the
   configuration already known to be broken, and a green result there would mean nothing.
   The costs Ayo accepted with it: the Slice 2 deploy had to go to production first (it was
   owed anyway), an EAS build was needed to carry Slice 2's i18n, and the test notifications
   are real production rows reaching only the two admin accounts, since the app is not on
   the stores. All such rows are deleted at the end of the slice.
6. **The audio field test is held to the Bluetooth bar**: a real 15+ minute continuous
   listen with the screen locked, over Bluetooth.

### Taken by Claude, from the repo's own written rules

7. **The producer is one new job, `activity-notices`, on the ADR 0016 pattern, not
   triggers.** ADR 0016's reason for `event-notices` applies verbatim ("a trigger-fired call
   is silence when it fails"); `21` §5 forbids an outbox by name ("derives its work from
   live state rather than an outbox"); and ADR 0022's "the insert IS the claim on a send"
   means a trigger-written row that nothing pushes would break the invariant. All three
   sources are queryable from live state with no new columns:
   `prayer_intercessions.prayed_at`, `glory_reactions.created_at`, and the posts' own
   `status` / `moderated_at`.

### Flagged, needs Ayo before Slice 2's SQL

8. **A removal has no author-facing reason to show.** `moderateItem` requires
   `rejectionReason` on a *reject* and `moderationNote` on a *remove*, and the note is
   deliberately unreadable by the author. So decision 3 as stated sends a removal
   notification whose destination shows the member a removed post and no explanation.
   Working assumption, to be confirmed: the safeguarding boundary wins, MY-POSTS shows its
   generic removed state, and **`09` line 82 is amended** from "author notified with reason"
   to "author notified, without disclosing the moderator's note". The alternative is a
   second, author-facing reason field on removals, which is more work for leaders every
   time and was not what W2.7 designed.

### Slice 2 · The producers (finding 1) · backend

One migration, one edge function, one pgTAP file, `deno test` for the core.

- `activity_notice_batch(clock)`: the "who is due" function, three arms, each gated on the
  column `15`'s tier table names (`prayer_activity`, `testimony_activity`, none for
  moderation), each **derived from live state**, each with its own dedupe key:
  - **prayed**: one per `prayer_intercessions` row reaching `state='prayed'`; recipient is
    the prayer's author; key `prayed:<intercession_id>`.
  - **glory**: per testimony per hour, count = the glories in that hour, after a settle
    window; key `glory:<testimony_id>:<hour>`.
  - **moderation**: on the transition into `approved` / `rejected` / `removed`; key carries
    `moderated_at`, so a re-approval after an edit correctly sends again.
- **Block suppression in either direction** on the two activity arms (`15`), and never on
  moderation (that is the church answering the member's own act, not activity between two
  people). There is deliberately no `is_blocked_with()` helper in this repo
  (`20260720220000:108`), so the clause is written out, as elsewhere.
- Never notify a member about their own act (self-Glory, praying for your own request).
- Follows every job convention: lease, grid-anchored window, clock as an argument,
  `deliver_notifications` as the only door, healthchecks ping, FAILURE on undeliverable
  push, `cron.schedule` through `jobs.invoke_edge_function` in the job's own migration.
- `15` amended: the per-hour collapse, and the tier table's Personal-activity row gaining
  its producer.

**Two things worth carrying past this item.**

*A removal could not keep the promise `09` made about it.* `moderateItem` requires
`rejectionReason` on a reject and `moderationNote` on a remove, and the note is invisible to
the author by column privilege (`20260803140000`) precisely because it is the safeguarding
record. So "author notified with reason" was unimplementable as written. The resolution came
from the app rather than from the spec: `MyPostCard.tsx` already states the rule in a comment
("rejected is a conversation the author can answer, removed is not") and MY-POSTS already
ships the right words in four languages, offering the author a **person** instead of a
reason. `moderation.removed` says the same thing. The near-miss worth remembering: reusing
`moderation.changes_needed` would have told a member whose post was taken down after review
to go and edit it, which is the one thing that must not happen, and it would have passed
every test that existed.

*A standard was consulted and deliberately not followed, per CLAUDE.md's "never silently
deviate".* `~/.claude/standards/backend.md` says "when a DB write and an event must both
happen, use a transactional outbox". That rule addresses DUAL writes that can drift; here the
notification is derived from the source rows themselves, so there is no second write to drift
and `21` §5's "derives its work from live state rather than an outbox" is strictly stronger.
No change proposed to the standard: it is right about the case it describes.

- **The exit criterion, live.** A ministry broadcast produced recipients in BOTH branches
  (Oami [Glasgow] and Ayo [Berlin]); a Glasgow branch broadcast produced exactly one
  delivery row, `in_app` to the Glasgow member, and **nothing at all** for the Berlin
  device, confirmed both in `broadcast_recipients` and in `broadcast_deliveries`. That is
  `18`'s "ministry-wide reaches all branches; branch stays in-branch" demonstrated on real
  infrastructure with real accounts, and it is the same claim Slice 1 added the missing
  pgTAP assertion for.
- **The delivery ledger's two shapes**, in production: the ministry broadcast wrote
  `in_app` × 2 (one per member, born `sent`) and `push` × 1 (one per DEVICE, and only one
  device exists).
- **The payload privacy rule.** The `prayer` notification's `params` is `{}`. Not the
  request, not a word of it, not who prayed (`15`, `20`).
- **The per-hour Glory collapse and its hour-boundary cap**, decided 2026-08-29 and observed
  the same day: the Glory landed at **14:00:01 UTC**, exactly the hour boundary. The
  15-minute settle would have held it to 14:09; the cap fired first. The batch function was
  also asked directly and answered `due_now = 0, due_at_hour_boundary = 1`.
- **Plural rules per language**: "**1 person** said Glory", singular, from `Intl.PluralRules`.
- **Per-category foreground behaviour**: the `prayer` push arrived while the app was
  FOREGROUNDED and went to the tray with no banner, which is what `15` specifies for
  activity.
- **The event remembers the plan it announced**: rescheduling 17:30 → 17:15 left
  `announced_starts_at_local` at 17:30, and the job derived a `moved` notice carrying the
  new time, sitting in the shade beside the original posting that still read 17:30.
- **Milestones are awarded on approval**, not submission (W2.8's decision), confirmed in
  production: `first_testimony` and `first_prayer` both fired.

#### One defect found: issue #213

A pending milestone modal is drawn **over a notification's deep-link destination**. The
`first_prayer` milestone, raised by the prayer tap and left undismissed, was re-presented on
top of EVENT-DETAIL after tapping an unrelated `event_change` notification. The deep link

#### Deviations from the plan as written, and why

- **The dashboard was not used** to compose broadcasts or create events. It would have
  needed a local dashboard pointed at production holding `SUPABASE_SECRET_KEY`, plus an
  email OTP and a TOTP from Ayo, to re-test a composer W3.5 already proved. The tier under
  test here is DELIVERY, so the broadcasts were inserted directly in `sending` state with
  distinct `author_id` and `approved_by` (the four-eyes CHECK satisfied honestly, not
  bypassed) and the events written directly. The composer UI is therefore NOT re-verified
  by this run, and does not need to be.
- **The moderation decisions were made in SQL**, for the same reason: the notification is
  derived from the row's state by the job, not triggered by the dashboard, so the same
  UPDATE proves the same thing without a second OTP round-trip.
- **The German render was not exercised.** The signed-in profile is `en` and switching
  `profiles.language` in production to check one string was not worth the write. Template
  rendering per language is covered by `deno test` across all four languages, and W3.3
  verified German on device.
- **The pref-off case was not exercised per tier.** Suppression is asserted in pgTAP for
  every gated tier (`040`, `041`, `043`, `046`, `051`), including the mutation checks that
  prove those assertions bite. Re-testing it through the device would prove nothing the
  database does not already prove more completely.

#### The last two tiers

- **`rsvp_reminder` landed at 15:11:01 UTC**, on the tick, with
  `params = {"event": "Midweek Prayer Gathering"}`: the title the church published itself
  and nothing else. Predicted before the wait rather than after, by asking the batch
  function directly: the 15:11 run owed exactly 1 and the 16:11 run owed 0, which is the
  grid-anchored hourly window doing its job (one send, not two).
- **`service_reminder` landed 2026-08-30 at 08:01:01 UTC**, which is 10:01 on the Berlin
  clock, an hour before the real 11:00 Sunday service and on the `1,16,31,46` grid tick.
  Nothing was staged for it: it fired off the seeded `branch_services` row against
  production, which makes it the only tier in the matrix that was OBSERVED rather than
  provoked: every other one, the RSVP reminder included, was staged by creating the content
  it reports on. `params = {"branch": "AGBC Lighthouse Berlin"}`, deep link `/home`.

  Worth recording WHY the 11:00 service produces an 08:01 UTC send, because it is `02`'s
  wall-clock rule paying off: the service is stored as `11:00` + `Europe/Berlin`, not as an
  instant, so it stays at 11:00 on the congregation's clock all year and the UTC moment
  moves with the season (09:00 UTC on CEST, 10:00 UTC on CET). Had it been stored as a fixed
  instant, the service would appear to jump an hour when the clocks change in October.

#### Cleanup, done 2026-08-29

Every row this slice created was deleted and the counts verified back to their pre-test
state: testimonies, prayers, glory reactions, intercessions, events, RSVPs, broadcasts and
deliveries all 0. The four pre-existing `notifications` (service reminders from 23 and 26
August) were preserved by scoping the delete to rows created after the run began.

**One piece of residue was nearly missed and is worth the note.** The run awarded Ayo the
`first_testimony` and `first_prayer` MILESTONES, which survived the first sweep because
milestones are server-owned and keyed `unique(profile_id, kind)`. Left in place they would
have silently stolen the real celebration: the next time he genuinely posts a first
testimony, no milestone would fire. Both were deleted. The general lesson for any future
production test: deleting the content does not undo what the content EARNED, and derived
server-owned rows (milestones, streaks, counters) have to be swept separately.
