# W4.18 · A message sent once arrives once

Opened 2026-09-16, after the contact form was found delivering duplicates on the day it
started working at all (see `docs/runbooks/credentials.md`, function secrets, for why it had
never worked). Ayo chose the wide scope on 2026-09-16: all three surfaces, worst first.

**Read this before touching any submit path in the app.** The rule it lands is not about the
contact form; it is about every write that left the write queue.

---

## 1. The defect, stated once

`apps/mobile/src/lib/fetchWithTimeout.ts:9` puts a 10-second abort on every supabase-js call,
and supabase-js reports that abort as a fetch-level failure indistinguishable from having no
network. Several surfaces then tell the member **the write did not happen**, and invite them
to try again.

That claim is a guess, and on a timeout it is frequently wrong: the request reached the
server, the server did the work, and only the answer was lost. The member retries, and the
thing happens twice.

**Proven, not theorised.** On 2026-09-16 one message typed once into CONTACT arrived twice at
`hello@agbcglobal.com`, one minute apart, both visible in Cloudflare's Email Routing activity
log. The app had called the first attempt a failure while Resend had already accepted it.

**The app already solves this correctly everywhere else, and that is the useful finding.**
`lib/writeQueue` plus `state/writeQueueHandlers.ts` make every queued handler idempotent, and
`outcomeFor` (`writeQueueHandlers.ts:44`) deliberately treats a code-less error, exactly what
a 10-second abort produces, as `retry` and sends again. That is only safe because each handler
sits behind a unique constraint: `attendance_profile_date_unique`, `rsvps_event_profile_unique`,
the `saved_items` primary key, `sermon_notes_profile_sermon_key`, and the rest. The queue's own
comment (`lib/writeQueue/index.ts:110`) says it outright: two concurrent passes would send
every write twice, and idempotency makes that survivable.

So the exposed writes are **precisely the ones that opted out of the queue without taking the
idempotency half with them**. Each opt-out is documented and each was right on its own terms
(`interest.ts:8`, `useAskToJoin.ts:17`, `notes.ts:25`, `markAnswered.ts:14`, `delete.tsx:39`).
None of them replaced what the queue was providing.

**A busy flag is not the fix, and this is the trap.** Every exposed surface already disables
its button while in flight (`Button.tsx:75`, and the busy flags at `ComposeFlow.tsx:157`,
`contact.tsx:315`, `RegistrationSheet.tsx:118`, `delete.tsx:323`). Those stop a double tap.
They do nothing about the retry, because the retry is the behaviour the error copy asks for.

---

## 2. Slices, ordered by harm rather than by discovery

### Slice 1 · The account-deletion screen stops claiming nothing happened

**The worst of the three**, and the reason the ordering is not the order the bugs were found
in. `app/settings/delete.tsx:96` catches every failure and shows
`settings:delete.failed`: "We couldn't delete your account. **Nothing has changed.** Check
your connection and try again." The comment above it states the reasoning plainly: "what they
need to know is the same either way: nothing has happened."

On a timeout that is false. `erase_profile()` commits the whole of `16`'s reach in one
transaction, so a lost response after a committed transaction leaves the account erased,
`signOut()` at `delete.tsx:85` never reached, the member still signed in locally on a deleted
account, and the screen telling them nothing happened. This is the GDPR erasure path and a
Play Store requirement.

**No migration, no function change, and the reason is the nicest thing in this item.**
`erase_profile` takes the profile row `for update` where `deleted_at is null` and raises
`no_data_found` with `no live account to erase` when there is none
(`20260901160000_the_erasure_itself.sql:112`). **A second call is therefore its own
confirmation**: that specific refusal is proof the first call succeeded. Nothing new has to be
built to find out the truth; the existing door already answers the question when asked twice.

Three outcomes, each with copy that is true of it:

| What came back | What it means | What the member is told |
|---|---|---|
| success, or `no_data_found` on the confirming call | the account is erased | the existing success path: sign out, `settings:delete.done`, back to `/` |
| a definite server refusal (the last-admin rule, `raise_exception`) | nothing happened | the existing `settings:delete.failed`, which is TRUE here |
| the confirming call also failed at transport level | **unknown** | new copy: we could not confirm, do not assume either way |

Two things to get right while building it:

- `delete.tsx:79` does `throw new Error(rpcError.message)`, which **discards the Postgres error
  code**. The classification needs the code, so the error object has to survive to the
  classifier. This is the first thing to change and it is easy to miss, because the code reads
  as if it is already handling the error.
- The confirming call is a second *real* attempt at deletion, not a read. That is safe (the
  function refuses the second erasure) and it is also correct: the member typed the confirm
  word, ticked the box and pressed Delete, so fulfilling that intent if the first attempt
  genuinely failed is what they asked for.

The decision goes in a pure classifier beside the screen, in the shape `mapComposeError` and
`askErrors.ts` already use here, so it is testable without a client to mock.

### Slice 2 · Testimony and prayer stop double-posting, and stop lying about why

`ComposeFlow.tsx:391` (`testimonies.insert`) and `:399` (`prayers.insert`) have no unique
constraint and no reconciliation, so a retry after a timeout publishes a second copy. A
duplicate here is worse than a duplicate email: it is a public post, it goes through
moderation twice, and the whole congregation sees it. `composeErrors.ts:39` maps the abort to
`family:errorOffline`, "That didn't send. Your draft is saved, so try again when you're back
online", and the retry is a single press because the member stays on the consent stage with
the tick still set (`ComposeFlow.tsx:308`).

**A standalone bug rides inside this one.** A testimony composed from an answered prayer
carries `from_prayer_id` and is protected by the partial unique
`testimonies_one_live_answer_per_prayer` (`20260804160000…sql:50`). After a
timed-out-but-succeeded insert the retry raises **23505**, which `mapComposeError`
(`composeErrors.ts:23`) does not recognise: it is neither `23514`, nor `23503`, nor the empty
code, so it falls through to `errorGeneric`, "please try again". **The author can press Post
for ever and will never be told their testimony is already published.** Compare
`interest.ts:29` and `moderation.ts:78`, which both handle 23505 correctly.

Shape of the fix, to be settled when the slice starts: the rows already default
`gen_random_uuid()`, so a client-minted id turns the insert into an upsert on the primary key
and makes the retry harmless by construction, which is the same move the queue makes
everywhere else. The alternative, a reconciliation read before re-inserting, is weaker
(it races) and is the fallback if the id path runs into RLS or the guard triggers.

### Slice 3 · The contact form and the registration sheet

The bug that started the item, and the least harmful of the three: a duplicate email in a
staff inbox.

**Two callers, not one**, and they must be fixed together or the duplicate stays live on the
second: `app/contact.tsx:107` (CONTACT) and `features/academy/registrationContact.ts:52` (the
Academy's "Email us about this registration" sheet). Both carry their own copy of the same
`machineCode` helper and their own error mapping, and the two copies already differ. One
shared sender replaces both.

Resend supports an `Idempotency-Key` header, unique per request, retained 24 hours, 256
characters maximum (verified against their API reference on 2026-09-16). The key is minted
with `Crypto.randomUUID()` from `expo-crypto`, already a dependency and already linked into
both dev clients via `features/family/photo.ts:222`, so no EAS rebuild is needed.

**The key is tied to the CONTENT, not to the tap and not to the screen.** Tied to the tap, a
retry sends a new key and duplicates as before. Tied to the screen, this happens: the send
fails, the member rephrases, presses Send, and Resend recognises the key and silently discards
the rewritten message. So the key persists while the submitted text is unchanged and is minted
afresh when the member edits. That rule is the thing most likely to be got wrong later, so it
carries its own test.

**The key is optional on the function side, permanently.** Edge functions deploy on a
`supabase-deploy.yml` dispatch and the app half waits for a store release, so builds 22 and
23, which are in members' hands now and send no key, must keep working.

Also in this slice, carried from the original bug: `captureEdgeError` on the `not_configured`
branch of `contact-form/index.ts:82`, which returns before the existing try/catch and is why
three weeks of dropped messages raised nothing anywhere.

### Slice 4 · The rule, written down where the next person will find it

The board entry in `25`, the CONTACT line in `04` §145, and an ADR. The ADR earns its place
because the rule generalises: **a write that leaves the write queue takes the queue's
idempotency obligation with it.** Without that written down, the next opt-out repeats this.

## 3. Both budgets are 10 seconds, and the client always loses the tie

`fetchWithTimeout.ts:9` aborts at 10 000 ms. `contact-form/index.ts:29` gives the Resend call
its own 10 000 ms. The client's clock starts before the function has even booted, so it always
expires first, which is why a slow send can only ever surface as the offline message and never
as "didn't send".

Narrowing that window is worth doing and is **not** a substitute for any slice above: it
reduces how often the wrong copy appears, and changes nothing about what happens when it does.
It rides slice 3, and it needs a per-call override in `fetchWithTimeout`, because supabase-js's
own `invoke({ timeout })` cannot help: our wrapper is installed as the client's `global.fetch`
(`lib/supabase.ts:30`) and always applies the shorter of the two.

## 4. Verification

Unit level per slice: jest for each pure classifier and for the content-keyed mint rule, deno
for the function's key handling, and the existing fake-timer harness in
`lib/__tests__/fetchWithTimeout.test.ts` for the budget change.

**None of that proves any of it**, for the reason W4.5 recorded: every layer passed alone and
the halves had never met. Each slice ends on the device, against production:

- Slice 1: not drivable without deleting a real account. It is verified by forcing the
  classifier's three branches in tests and by a hand walk of the screen against a seeded
  account on the LOCAL stack, with the deletion actually performed there.
- Slice 2: post a testimony, force the timeout, retry, and confirm one post rather than two.
  Then the linked-testimony case, which must say something true instead of "try again".
- Slice 3: let the function go cold, send once, expect success rather than an error. Then
  force the failure path, retry, and confirm exactly one email arrives.
