> dashboard-only. This item spans the app as well, which is why it has its own doc.

> **Prerequisite landed:** PR #101 (`fix: a branch is assigned, never self-assigned`) closed

## Why this is one item

Role assignment and branch moves look like two features and are one. Both change who holds
authority over which branch, both need the same admin write path, both need the same audit
record, and both are refused today by the same trigger. Ayo chose to build them together
rather than sequence them (2026-07-29), on the grounds that the shared pieces should be
built once.

Role assignment is the part that unblocks other work: `25` pulled it forward from Phase B
because there is currently one admin and no leaders, so the moderation queue built in
slices 2 and 3 is a single-user tool and the branch scoping W2.5/W2.6 depend on has never
been exercised by a real leader.

## What exists today (after PR #101)

- `profiles_guard` refuses three things: an owner writing their own `role` (admins
  included, bootstrap exempt), an owner writing their own `branch_id` once `onboarded_at`
  is set, and any of the older immutables. Its privileged bypass asks
  `caller_is_admin_live()`, not the JWT claim.
- **There is no write path to another member's profile at all.** The only UPDATE policy on
  `profiles` is `members update their own profile` (`id = auth.uid()`), so an admin
  updating someone else returns `UPDATE 0` rather than a refusal. Asserted in pgTAP `018`
  test 9. Everything below has to bring its own path.
- `in_bootstrap_promote()` is the established pattern for "this write is server-owned even
  though it carries a member's uid": a transaction-local GUC the guard checks. Mirror it,
  do not invent a second mechanism.
- No audit table exists. Moderation records `moderated_by` / `moderated_at` on the row.
- Push and the Notification Center are **Phase 3** (`18`). `devices` and
  `notification_prefs` exist from W0.x; no notification code exists in the app.
- `nav.people` is already a dimmed row in the dashboard rail (`src/copy/en.ts`).

## Decided with Ayo, 2026-07-29

Recorded with the reasoning, because these are the decisions a future session will be
tempted to re-open.

1. **The branch a member is JOINING approves the move.** Berlin's leader decides; Glasgow's
   is told. They are the one who will see the person on Sunday, whose counts and reminders
   change, and who can verify the person is actually attending. It also avoids giving a
   leader the power to refuse someone leaving, which is the wrong shape in a church.
2. **The 48 hours is an expectation, not a hold.** The move lands the moment it is
   approved. The app says it usually takes up to 48 hours. Churn is prevented by a
   **90-day cooldown after a COMPLETED move** and by allowing **one open request at a
   time**, not by delaying approved moves.
3. **A rejection tells the member nothing beyond the outcome.** A private note is required
   for the ministry record; the member sees a neutral "not approved, speak to your branch
   leader". Chosen over the two-field split used for content moderation because the reasons
   most needing a record are the ones that must not be handed back (`20`), and a leader
   should not be choosing which box to type into under time pressure. The consequence is
   that the member-facing copy carries real weight: it explains nothing, so it must at
   least be kind and point at a human.
4. **A leader's approved move drops them to member.** Leadership is authority over one
   branch's content and does not travel. An admin can grant leadership of the new branch in
   the same action when that is the intent, so the common case stays one step.
5. **The admin is the fallback approver**: immediately when the destination branch has no
   leader, and after 48 hours when it does. Mirrors the 48-hour moderation escalation `17`
   already defines, so the product has one escalation idea. It is also what makes the 48
   hours quoted to members honest rather than dependent on one person being available.
6. **An admin finds people by exact email address only.** No partial matching, so nobody can
   probe which addresses belong to the ministry, and no member list is ever rendered
   (`20`, data minimisation).
7. **A general privileged-action log, built to `17`'s wording once.** Role and branch
   changes write to it now; moderation decisions, restores and later broadcasts migrate on
   as those surfaces are touched.
8. **Step-up re-auth on role changes only.** `17` names role assignment explicitly.
   Approving a branch move is closer to a moderation decision, and re-challenging a leader
   on every queue item is how queues stop getting cleared.
9. **Admins may set their own home branch.** It grants them nothing, since an admin
   moderates every branch regardless, and locking it would leave the sole admin unable to
   correct their own without a migration. Note this needs **no code**: the privileged bypass
   already returns before the branch check. It needs a test pinning it as intended, not
   accidental.
10. **On erasure, the audit record survives and the identity does not.** The row keeps
    actor, action, timestamp and a tombstone in place of the deleted person's identifying
    data, so "who granted authority, and when" stays answerable while the member is
    genuinely gone.
11. **The member learns the outcome from the app, not a notification.** State on the Profile
    screen, plus a quiet dismissible line on Home while a request is open. Push replaces the
    banner in Phase 3.
12. **A leader learns of waiting requests from the dashboard**, as a count beside the
    moderation one. Nothing is emailed. The admin fallback in (5) is the safety net.
13. **Audit retention is 7 years**, aligned with charity governance record-keeping, recorded
    in `20`'s retention schedule and to be stated in the privacy notice alongside the
    erasure carve-out in (10). The purge job is deferred rather than forgotten: nothing
    approaches 7 years for a long time, and `21` §5 carries the pointer.
14. **The source branch's leader sees decided requests as read-only history.** They are told
    a member left, after the fact, in the same place they already work. They do not approve
    and cannot block.
15. **New tables follow the existing `gen_random_uuid()` pattern.** `database.md` prefers
    time-ordered UUIDv7 and PG 17.6 has no native function for it. One table diverging is
    worse than a consistent convention, so the debt is accepted deliberately and PG18's
    native `uuidv7()` is the moment to migrate every table at once. Recorded as a decision
    so it reads as accepted debt rather than an oversight.

### Consequence of (14): the private note moves

Decision 14 collides with decision 3. Letting the source leader see the request row means
letting them see every column on it, because RLS is row-level: a crafted PostgREST select
would return `decision_note`, which is supposed to be private to the ministry record.

So **the note does not live on `branch_change_requests` at all.** It is written into
`privileged_actions`, which is already admin-only, immutable and the right home for a
ministry record. The request table then holds nothing private (who moved where, and when),
and its policies stay simple enough to be obviously correct.

The trade-off, stated rather than buried: **the leader who wrote the note cannot re-read
it.** Only admins can. That differs from content moderation, where a leader can see their
own `moderation_note`. It does not change what the MEMBER sees, which was the substance of
decision 3, and it removes a column-level privacy problem that RLS is the wrong tool for.
Revisit only if leaders actually ask to see their own decision history.

## How it behaves

Written as what actually happens, in order, because the tables and policies below are only
the means. If any of this reads wrong to a person who knows the ministry, the design is wrong
and the schema does not get to argue.

### Right now, before any of this is built

The database rule shipped ahead of the screens (PR #101), so this is the live behaviour today:

- A member opens Settings. There is no way to change their branch, and there never was: the
  only branch picker is in onboarding. So **nobody notices anything**, which is why shipping
  the rule early was safe.
- The `BRANCH-SWITCH` sheet on Home still lets anyone browse Berlin's services and events
  instantly, guest or member. That never needed permission and still does not.
- If something did try to write a member's branch, the database refuses it with "a branch
  change is approved by a leader or admin, not self-assigned" and nothing changes.
- A leader who tries the old trick, editing their own profile to Glasgow to get at Glasgow's
  queue, gets the same refusal and sees exactly zero new items.
- Ayo, as admin, can still change his own home branch, and it works.

### A member moves from Glasgow to Berlin

1. They open Settings, then Profile, and tap their home branch. Or they tap the branch chip on
   Home and choose "Make this my home branch" on the sheet. **Both routes reach the same
   place**; the sheet is not a shortcut past anything.
2. A confirm sheet says plainly what they are asking for and that a leader confirms it, usually
   within 48 hours. They confirm.
3. Their Profile row now reads **Berlin, awaiting confirmation**, with a Cancel option. Home
   carries a quiet dismissible line so they do not forget they asked. Their branch is **still
   Glasgow** for everything that matters: reminders, attendance timezone, "My branch" feeds.
4. They can still browse anywhere. Browsing was never gated.
5. If they tap the branch row again, they see the same pending state, not a fresh picker. **One
   open request at a time.** To ask for Emmen instead they cancel first.
6. Berlin's leader approves. **The moment they do, the member is in Berlin**: no waiting out the
   48 hours, because the 48 hours was always a description of how long a human takes, not a hold.
7. The member sees Berlin next time they open the app. Their streak is untouched, their old
   testimonies stay in Glasgow's history, and their attendance record does not move. Nothing
   re-buckets, because `service_date` was fixed when they tapped "I'm here".
8. If they ask to move again three weeks later, they are told, without being scolded, when they
   can ask again. **90 days from the completed move.**

### The same member, refused

1. Steps 1 to 5 are identical.
2. Berlin's leader refuses, and must type a reason to do it.
3. The member sees a short, kind message: the request was not approved, here is Berlin's name
   and its contact address if they would like to talk to someone. **They are not told why, and
   they are not told who decided.** Both are deliberate, because the reasons that most need
   recording are safeguarding ones.
4. They can dismiss it, and **they may ask again immediately.** A refusal starts no cooldown, so
   if the leader got it wrong it is fixable the same day rather than costing the member months.
5. The reason the leader typed exists, in the audit log, readable by an admin. Not by the
   member, and not by the leader afterwards either.

### Nobody gets round to it

- Berlin **has a leader** who does not open the dashboard: at 48 hours it becomes available to an
  admin, who can approve or refuse it. That is what makes "usually within 48 hours" honest
  rather than dependent on one person's week.
- Berlin **has no leader at all**, which is true of every branch today: an admin can act on it
  immediately. No waiting for a person who does not exist.

### A leader's dashboard visit

1. They sign in and pass their second factor as usual.
2. The rail shows their moderation count and, next to it, a count of people asking to join
   their branch. Both get seen on the same visit, which is the point.
3. They open the queue and approve someone. **No extra code is asked for.** Re-challenging on
   every item is how queues stop getting cleared.
4. Lower down, separately, they see who has left their branch recently. Read-only. They are
   told, after the fact, and cannot block someone leaving.
5. They cannot see a move out of their branch while it is still being considered, and they never
   see anyone's refusal reason, including moves out.

### A leader moves branch

1. They ask the same way a member does.
2. It goes to an admin, not to a leader, because a leader approving their own kind of move is
   how scope leaks.
3. When the admin approves, **the leader becomes an ordinary member of Berlin.** Their Glasgow
   moderation rights end. They do not arrive in Berlin holding Berlin's queue.
4. If the intent was that they lead Berlin, the admin grants that in the same action, so it is
   one step and a deliberate one.
5. Glasgow may now have no leader. The dashboard says so rather than hiding it.

### Ayo promotes someone to leader

1. People, then the role screen. He types the person's **full email address.** Partial matching
   does not exist, so there is no way to sweep for which addresses belong to the ministry, and
   no list of members is ever put on screen.
2. The person appears: name, branch, current role.
3. He chooses "leader" and picks their branch.
4. **He is asked for a fresh code from his authenticator**, because handing out authority is the
   irreversible act in this whole feature.
5. It is done, and it is in the audit log with his name against it.
6. Things he cannot do: change his own role, or demote the last remaining admin. He can change
   his own home branch freely, since it grants him nothing.
7. From that moment the new leader can sign in and clear their branch's queue, which is the
   whole reason this slice was pulled forward.

### A branch is archived

Members of that branch are asked on next launch to pick a new home branch, and **that one goes
through immediately.** No approval, no cooldown. There is no branch left to stay in and no leader
to ask, so making them queue would be absurd.

### Someone asks to be deleted

Their account and content go. The audit rows recording that they were once made a leader stay,
with their identity stripped out. So "who appointed this person, and when" survives, and the
person does not. Written into the privacy notice rather than left as a surprise.

## Data model

### `public.privileged_actions` (new, append-only)

| column | type | notes |
|---|---|---|
| id | `bigint generated always as identity` primary key | Internal-only and append-only, so a monotonic key is right and the UUID question does not arise (`database.md`: bigint internally is a valid pattern where there is no external id) |
| actor_id | `uuid null references public.profiles(id) on delete set null` | Null means the actor's account was erased, or the action was server-owned (a job, a migration) |
| target_id | `uuid null references public.profiles(id) on delete set null` | Null after erasure; see `target_redacted_at` |
| target_redacted_at | `timestamptz null` | Stamped by the deletion job when it drops the identity, so a null `target_id` is distinguishable from "never had one" |
| action | `public.privileged_action` (enum) | `role_granted`, `role_revoked`, `branch_assigned`, `branch_request_approved`, `branch_request_rejected`. An enum rather than a lookup table: the set is small, internal, and every value is code-branched. Adding one is a single `ALTER TYPE ... ADD VALUE`, non-blocking since PG12 |
| before | `jsonb null` | Only the fields that changed. Read as a unit, never joined, which is the legitimate use for JSON per `database.md` |
| after | `jsonb null` | Same |
| request_id | `uuid null references public.branch_change_requests(id) on delete set null` | Links a branch action back to the request that caused it |
| note | `text null` | The private ministry record, required when a request is rejected. Lives here rather than on the request, so that decision 14 can give the source leader row access without exposing it. Admin-readable only |
| occurred_at | `timestamptz not null default now()` | |

- **Immutable, enforced twice.** No UPDATE or DELETE policy, plus a `BEFORE UPDATE OR
  DELETE` trigger that raises. `17` requires immutability, and a missing policy is not the
  same as a refusal once a service-role path exists.
- **Explicit REVOKE is mandatory, not optional.** Supabase's `ALTER DEFAULT PRIVILEGES`
  grants ALL on new public tables (found the hard way in `015` test 4, issue #96). The
  migration must `revoke all on public.privileged_actions from anon, authenticated;` and
  then grant only `select` to `authenticated`. Assert the grant count in pgTAP.
- `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY`. SELECT policy: `caller_is_admin_live()` only. No client
  INSERT policy; rows arrive only from the trigger below.
- Indexes: `(target_id, occurred_at desc)`, `(actor_id, occurred_at desc)`,
  `(action, occurred_at desc)`. Both FK columns indexed per `database.md`.
- **Not partitioned.** Privileged actions are a handful per month, so time partitioning
  buys nothing yet. Naming the ceiling as `database.md` asks: revisit the moment moderation
  decisions migrate onto this table, which turns it into thousands of rows a year.
- **Retention is an open question** (see below). It needs a stated period in `20`.

### `public.branch_change_requests` (new)

| column | type | notes |
|---|---|---|
| id | `uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid()` | See the UUID note under Open questions |
| profile_id | `uuid not null references public.profiles(id) on delete cascade` | Trigger-forced to `auth.uid()` on insert |
| from_branch_id | `uuid not null references public.branches(id)` | Trigger-forced to the requester's current branch, never taken from the client |
| to_branch_id | `uuid not null references public.branches(id)` | |
| status | `public.branch_request_status` (enum) | `pending`, `approved`, `rejected`, `cancelled` |
| decided_at | `timestamptz null` | Server-set. A timestamp discloses nothing about identity, so it is safe on a row the member reads |
| created_at / updated_at | `timestamptz not null default now()` | |

- `check (from_branch_id <> to_branch_id)`.
- **One open request at a time**, as a constraint rather than application logic:
  `create unique index on public.branch_change_requests (profile_id) where status = 'pending'`.
- **The cooldown is derived, not stored.** It reads the most recent `approved` request for
  that profile and refuses a new one inside 90 days. No `branch_changed_at` column, because
  that would be a second source for a fact the requests table already holds (the "one
  visible fact, one owner" convention). It also falls out correctly for a member who has
  never moved: no approved row, no cooldown, and the onboarding choice is not a request.
- `FORCE ROW LEVEL SECURITY`. Policies:
  - a member selects and inserts their own, and may update their own only to set
    `status = 'cancelled'` while `pending`;
  - a leader selects and updates where `to_branch_id` is their branch (**destination**, per
    decision 1);
  - a leader selects, but never updates, where `from_branch_id` is their branch **and
    `status <> 'pending'`** (decision 14: history after the fact, no ability to block
    someone leaving, and no visibility of a request still being considered);
  - an admin everything.
- **THE RULE FOR THIS TABLE, hit twice already in one design.** A column on a row the subject
  can read is disclosed to the subject, whatever the UI does with it. RLS is row-level, so
  there is no policy that hides a field. Two columns tried to live here and could not:
  - `decision_note`, because the source leader gets read access to the row (decision 14);
  - `decided_by`, because the MEMBER gets read access to their own row. Copy that carefully
    avoids naming the decider is worth nothing when the member can read the id straight off
    the row through PostgREST. Naming who refused someone is exactly the disclosure the
    no-reason rule in decision 3 exists to prevent, and it is worst in the safeguarding cases.

  Both now live in `privileged_actions` (`actor_id` and `note`), which is admin-read-only.
  What remains here is who asked, from where, to where, the status and the timestamps: **every
  column is safe for every reader above.** That is what makes these policies obviously correct
  rather than merely tested. Preserve it. If a field ever needs adding that is not safe for all
  four readers, it belongs in the audit log or behind a view, not here with a cleverer policy.

### `public.profiles`

No column changes. One addition:

- **An `AFTER UPDATE` trigger writes the audit row** whenever `role` or `branch_id`
  actually changes, on every path: an RPC, a migration, a direct SQL fix, the admin
  self-branch carve-out. Auditing belongs at the data layer, not inside each function that
  remembers to call it. This is the same reasoning already written into the `moderation_note`
  migration about making a rule structural rather than remembered, and it is what makes
  decision 9's carve-out auditable without a special case. `auth.uid()` still reads the
  caller inside a `SECURITY DEFINER` function, so the true actor is captured.

## Server surface

Two `SECURITY DEFINER` functions, because RLS has no path to another member's profile and
should not get a broad one.

**Hygiene for both** (`security.md`, plus the lesson in `015`): owned by `postgres`,
`set search_path = ''` with every reference schema-qualified, `revoke execute from public,
anon`, `grant execute to authenticated`, and authorization checked inside the function
rather than assumed from who can call it.

### `public.set_member_role(target uuid, new_role public.profile_role, new_branch uuid default null)`

- Refuses unless `caller_is_admin_live()`.
- Refuses `target = auth.uid()`. The guard already refuses this, but a clear error beats a
  trigger message surfacing through an RPC.
- Refuses demoting the **last** admin: `count(*) where role = 'admin' and deleted_at is
  null` must stay >= 1. Note this is currently unreachable via self-demotion, which is
  already impossible, so it only bites once a second admin exists. Build it anyway.
- Refuses unless the caller's session is `aal2`. Defence in depth behind the dashboard's
  step-up, not a replacement for it.
- Sets `role`, and `branch_id` when `new_branch` is supplied (only meaningful when granting
  `leader`), inside the `agbc.privileged_profile_write` transaction-local flag that
  `profiles_guard` recognises. Mirror `in_bootstrap_promote()` exactly; do not add a second
  mechanism.

### `public.decide_branch_request(request uuid, approve boolean, note text default null)`

- Loads the request. Refuses unless the caller `can_moderate_branch(to_branch_id)` or is a
  live admin. **The destination, not the source.**
- When the destination has a leader and the caller is an admin, allowed only once the
  request is 48 hours old (decision 5). When the destination has no leader, an admin may
  act immediately.
- Requires a non-empty `note` when rejecting, and writes it to `privileged_actions.note`,
  never to the request row (see the consequence of decision 14).
- On approve: sets `profiles.branch_id` under the flag; if the target holds `leader`, drops
  them to `member` (decision 4).
- Sets `decided_at` and `status` server-side; the DECIDER's identity goes only to
  `privileged_actions.actor_id`, never onto the request row. The audit row for the profile change
  itself comes from the trigger; this function records the `request_id` linkage and the note.
  A **rejection** changes no profile, so the trigger never fires for it: this function writes
  that audit row directly. Easy to miss, and it is the case where the note matters most.
- One transaction, so a half-applied move is not possible.

Both under **People**, which is already a dimmed rail row.

- **`/people`** (role assignment): an exact-email field, then the person (display name,
  branch, current role), a role choice, a branch choice shown only when granting `leader`, a

  which is the thing a leader would otherwise notice by a member quietly disappearing from

- **Rail**: a waiting count beside Moderation (decision 12). The count is the actionable

- Every route awaits `authorize()`; state-changing handlers call `isSameOrigin()` first.

## Derived rules and edge cases

- **Onboarding is untouched.** The first choice is free, is not a request, and starts no
  cooldown.
- **An archived branch bypasses all of this.** `17` module 5 already says archiving prompts
  members to choose a new home branch. There is no branch to stay in and no leader to ask,
  so that path is an admin-initiated assignment writing audit rows, not a request, and the
  cooldown does not apply.
- A pending request whose destination is archived is auto-cancelled.
- A rejection starts no cooldown (decision 2), so a leader's mistake is fixable the same day.
- **Streaks and history need no work.** `02` §streaks already states that `service_date` is
  immutable so "branch-timezone edits and home-branch changes never re-bucket history".
  Assert it rather than implement it.
- Approving a move for a leader of the SOURCE branch can leave that branch leaderless.
  Surface it, do not block it.
- **Zero admins is a real lockout.** If the sole admin's account is erased, nobody can grant
  roles. Break-glass is the existing `bootstrap_admins` table plus a migration. The durable
  fix is naming a second admin, which is already an open item and should happen before
  Founding Members.

## Acceptance criteria

1. An admin can promote a member to leader of a named branch, and that leader can then see
   and clear their branch's moderation queue.
2. A leader cannot call either RPC for a branch that is not theirs, and cannot call
   `set_member_role` at all.
3. A member cannot approve anything, cannot forge `profile_id` or `from_branch_id`, and
   cannot read another member's request or anything in `privileged_actions`.
4. A member's approved move changes their branch, and the member sees it in the app without
   signing out and back in.
5. A second request inside 90 days of a completed move is refused with copy that says when
   they can ask again. A request after a rejection is not refused.
6. A member cannot hold two open requests.
7. An approved move for a leader leaves them a member, unless the admin granted the new
   branch's leadership in the same action.
8. Every role and branch change, by any path including direct SQL, leaves exactly one
   `privileged_actions` row with the correct actor.
9. `privileged_actions` cannot be updated or deleted by anyone, and is readable only by
   admins.
10. An admin can change their own home branch, and it is audited.
11. The last admin cannot be demoted.
12. Erasing a member leaves their audit rows present, with the identity dropped and
    `target_redacted_at` stamped.
13. Role assignment requires a fresh TOTP code; approving a branch request does not.
14. The SOURCE branch's leader sees a decided move out of their branch, cannot act on it,
    cannot see it while it is still pending, and never sees the note. The DESTINATION
    leader's badge count is unaffected by history.
15. No leader, by any route including a hand-written PostgREST query, can read a rejection
    note. Only admins can.
16. A refused member, selecting their own request row directly rather than through the app,
    learns the status and the timestamp and **cannot learn who refused them**. The refusal
    copy names the branch and its published contact, never a person.
17. A rejection, which changes no profile and so fires no trigger, still produces exactly
    one audit row carrying the note.
18. Every new screen is diffed element-by-element against its approved frame, in both
    themes, and verified at the device's maximum font setting with the longest branch name
    selected (AGBC Lighthouse Berlin).

## Test plan

- **pgTAP `019`**: the RPC authorization matrix (member, leader of source, leader of
  destination, admin, anon), the cooldown, the one-open-request index, the trigger writing
  exactly one audit row per change, immutability of `privileged_actions`, its grant count,
  the erasure tombstone, the last-admin refusal, and the admin self-branch carve-out.
  Also, and these are the ones a happy-path suite would skip: a SOURCE leader selecting the
  base table directly and getting decided rows only (never a pending one), the same leader
  selecting `privileged_actions` and getting nothing, a refused MEMBER selecting their own row
  and finding no way to identify who refused them, and a rejection producing its audit row even
  though no profile changed and no trigger fired.
- **A column-inventory assertion on `branch_change_requests`**, in the spirit of `017`'s check
  that `moderation_note` is absent from the feed views: assert the table's column list matches
  the safe set exactly, so adding a sensitive column later fails a test rather than quietly
  disclosing itself to whichever reader the policies already admit.
  Namespace fixtures `t019-` and create the branches the test needs rather than counting
  seeded rows, per the ambient-data lesson.
  **Assert `has_function_privilege` for the negative cases; never invoke a function the test
  role lacks EXECUTE on.** Doing so segfaults the local backend (recorded in memory).
- **Dashboard Vitest**: `server` project for both routes with real tokens from the local
  stack, including the IDOR probes; `ui` project for the forms, the neutral not-found path,
  and axe. Do not assert focus that React or the router placed.
- **Mobile Jest/RNTL**: the four states, the confirm sheet, cancel, and each outcome state.
- **Manual**: the whole loop on a device against the local stack, since decision 11 means
  the outcome is only discoverable in-app.
